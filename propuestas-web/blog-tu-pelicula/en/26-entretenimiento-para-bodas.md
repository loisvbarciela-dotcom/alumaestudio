# Wedding entertainment: reception ideas beyond the photo booth

At every wedding there are moments when guests wait: while the couple have their photos taken, between courses, before the party begins. Handled well, those moments turn a correct wedding into an enjoyable one. **Wedding entertainment** is not about filling the day with activities, but about choosing two or three that suit you and your guests.

## During drinks and canapés

Drinks usually coincide with the couple's photo session, so guests spend a good while without you. Some ideas:

- **Live music:** a jazz trio, a string quartet or, in Galicia, a gaita (Galician bagpipes) that appears by surprise.
- **A stall with a story:** seafood, cheese or wine, with someone to explain what is being served.
- **Lawn games:** boules, skittles, wooden garden games. They work especially well with children and older guests.
- **A different guest book:** a typewriter, a map where each guest marks where they have come from, an audio message of congratulations.

## Beyond the photo booth

The photo booth with props is already at almost every wedding. Alternatives that still surprise:

- **A portrait photographer** with a simple backdrop and careful lighting: guests take home a proper portrait.
- **An instant camera on each table**, with an album where everyone sticks their photo and writes something.
- **A video-message corner**, where each guest records a few seconds for the couple.

## During the reception

### A cinematic premiere
One of the moments with the greatest impact is switching off the lights and screening a film. If the couple are also the protagonists of a love story in another era — a twenties ballroom, Ancient Rome, a space station — the whole room falls silent. It is what we do in [Your film](/en/your-film): a three- or four-minute piece created from one photograph of each of you.

### Games that embarrass nobody
A quiz about the couple, with questions on each table, answered in writing and read out at the end. Better if the prizes are symbolic.

### Short performances
A song, a monologue, a reading. What matters is that they are brief and do not pile up.

### Planned speeches
Limiting the number and length of speeches is, in itself, a way of entertaining: four two-minute speeches are enjoyed; two fifteen-minute ones are not.

## At the party

- **A first dance that becomes everyone's.**
- **A surprise entrance by the band** or a drumming group.
- **A moment of tradition**, such as the queimada with its *conxuro* (incantation) at Galician weddings.
- **An open bar with signature cocktails** named after places that matter to the couple.

## How to choose

Think about your guests:

- **If there are many children**, lawn games and a space of their own.
- **If there are many older guests**, live music and seated moments (such as a screening).
- **If there are many guests who do not know one another**, activities that mix the tables.

And think about the pace: no more than one element per moment of the day. Complementary ideas in [original ideas to surprise your wedding guests](/en/blog/ideas-originales-para-sorprender-en-una-boda).

## Frequently asked questions

### What entertainment works at every wedding?
Live music and shared moments, such as a screening at the reception, usually please all ages.

### When is the best time to screen a video at a wedding?
Before the speeches or after the main course, with the room seated and attentive. More detail in [which video to screen at a wedding](/en/blog/que-video-proyectar-en-una-boda).

### How long is the personalised film?
Between three and four minutes, a length that can be watched in full without anyone switching off.
