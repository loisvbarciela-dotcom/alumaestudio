# What video to show at a wedding: a guide to the most emotional moment of the reception

Few moments at a wedding hold as much attention as a screening. The lights go down, the room falls silent and for a few minutes every guest looks in the same direction. Well chosen, it is what people talk about the next day. Badly planned, it is ten minutes of blurry photos with the sound turned up too loud.

This guide gathers **wedding video ideas** for the reception, the best moment to show them and a list of things worth checking with the venue.

## Five kinds of video that work at a reception

### 1. The couple's photo montage
The classic: childhood photos, teenage photos and photos from the relationship, with a song in the background. It moves the family, though it looks a lot like the one at other weddings. If you choose it, keep it short and be demanding about the selection. Ideas for giving it a twist in [alternatives to the wedding photo montage](/es/blog/alternativas-al-montaje-de-fotos-de-boda).

### 2. Messages from people who can't be there
A grandfather who lives far away, a friend who is abroad. It works as an intimate moment and needs no more than two or three minutes.

### 3. The friends' video
Humour, anecdotes, perhaps a staged scene. Well judged, it gets the laughs; badly judged, it drags on and loses half the room.

### 4. “How we met”, told by the couple
A short interview with the couple. It is warm and simple to produce.

### 5. A film starring the bride and groom
The most cinematic option: a story of three or four minutes in which the couple live their love in another era or another world, created from two photos. It doesn't look back at the past: it turns it into cinema. It is what we do in [Your film](/es/tu-pelicula).

## When to show it

There is no fixed rule, but these moments tend to work best:

- **Before the speeches or just after the main course:** the room is seated, attentive and the dancing hasn't started yet.
- **Before the cake:** it works as a transition towards the end of the meal.
- **At the opening of the party**, if the video is energetic and you want to lead into the first dance.

Avoid showing it while a course is being served: the noise of the waiting staff and the conversation swallow the sound.

## Ideal length

Less is more. As a guide:

- Photo montages and friends' videos: **three to five minutes**.
- A film or narrative piece: **three or four minutes** is a length people watch all the way through without anyone checking their phone.

If you have several videos, it is better to spread them over two moments than to chain them together.

## Technical checklist to go through with the venue

- **Screen or projector:** size, position and whether it can be seen from every table.
- **Light:** can the room be darkened? At daytime weddings a projector loses a lot; an LED screen may be a better option.
- **Sound:** the audio should come through the venue's or the DJ's system, not a small speaker.
- **File format:** ask which format and resolution they accept and bring two copies on different media.
- **Test run:** the day before or that morning, with the final file.
- **Person in charge:** one person (not the couple) who is responsible for pressing play.
- **Plan B:** a laptop with the file and the right cable.

## How to introduce it

One sentence is enough. A friend or the master of ceremonies can introduce the video by saying who prepared it and why. If it is a film, giving it a title and reading the “credits” aloud adds solemnity: *“A film starring…”*

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a wedding video be?
Between three and five minutes is the best advice. Beyond that, it is hard to hold the room's attention.

### Is a projector or a screen better?
It depends on the light in the space. In bright rooms, or outdoors in daytime, an LED screen usually shows up better.

### Who should be in charge of the screening?
Someone you trust who isn't part of the couple, coordinated with the DJ or the venue.
