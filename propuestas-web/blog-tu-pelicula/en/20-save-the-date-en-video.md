# Save the date video: the most original way to announce your wedding

The *save the date* is your guests' first contact with your wedding. Before the formal invitation, a message that says ‘keep this date free’. It might be a card, a fridge magnet or a text message. More and more couples are turning it into a **save the date video**, and it makes sense: it can be shared in seconds and sets the tone for everything that follows.

## Why a video

- **It reaches everyone** through messaging, with no printers or postage.
- **It conveys the tone of the wedding:** elegant, playful, epic, intimate.
- **It gets shared:** guests forward it and talk about it.
- **It builds anticipation**, which is exactly what a *save the date* is for.

## When to send it

As a rule of thumb, send it well in advance, especially if guests have to travel or if the wedding falls in peak season. The formal invitation then confirms the details.

## What it should include

The essentials:

1. **Your names.**
2. **The date.**
3. **The city or venue**, even in general terms.
4. **A note that the formal invitation will follow.**

Everything else is style.

## Save the date video ideas

### The trailer
A video that imitates a film trailer: short shots, rising music, title cards (‘This summer…’, ‘A love story…’) and, at the end, the date as the release date. It is the most cinematic format and the one people talk about most.

### The making of the proposal
If you filmed the moment of the proposal, or have photos of it, a short montage that ends with the date.

### Home-made stop motion
Objects moving across a table until they spell out the date. Simple, handcrafted, charming.

### The place as the star
Shots of the place where you are marrying, with the date superimposed. It works very well for destination weddings.

### You in another era
From one photograph of each of you, a short film showing you in the twenties, in Ancient Rome or in outer space, ending with the date. It is the idea behind our [personalised film](/es/tu-pelicula), applied to the announcement.

## The trailer and the film: two pieces, one story

There is an option we are particularly fond of: have a personalised film made to premiere at the wedding, and use a fragment of a few seconds as your *save the date*. Guests receive the trailer months ahead and see the full film at the reception. The announcement and the big day are linked by the same story.

## Tips to make it work

- **Keep it short:** between 15 and 45 seconds.
- **Vertical or square**, if it will mostly be watched on a phone.
- **With subtitles:** many people will watch without sound.
- **The date, clear and at the end.**
- **A lightweight file**, so it can be sent by messaging without problems.

## Common mistakes

- Making it too long.
- Forgetting the city or the year.
- Using a song whose rights prevent it from being posted on some platforms.
- Sending it too late, when the formal invitation is already on its way.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a save the date video be?
Between 15 and 45 seconds. It is an announcement, not the film.

### Do we need to send it to every guest?
It is usually sent to those who will be on the final guest list, above all to those who have to arrange travel.

### Can the wedding film be used as a save the date?
Yes, with a short fragment as a trailer. The rest is kept for the premiere at the wedding.
