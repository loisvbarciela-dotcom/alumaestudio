# Your image and AI: consent, rights and the EU AI Act

When someone sends us two photographs to create a film, they are entrusting us with something very personal: their face. It is only natural to ask what happens to those photos, who can use the film and what the law says. This article explains it clearly.

*This text is for information only and is not legal advice. For specific cases, please consult a professional.*

## Your image belongs to you

In Spain, a person's image is protected as a fundamental right: nobody may capture, reproduce or publish it without their consent, except in very specific circumstances set out by law. A photograph of a face is also **personal data** under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), with everything that implies: a clear purpose, security and the right to ask for it to be erased.

With artificial intelligence, these rules do not disappear. They become more important, because the technology can create new images of a person.

## What the EU AI Act requires

The EU AI Act sets out **transparency** obligations for content generated or manipulated with AI that resembles real people. In simple terms: whoever shares that kind of content must make clear that it is artificial, where appropriate.

For a personalised film, this comes down to three commitments:

1. **Explicit consent** from the people who appear in it.
2. **Traceability** of what has been generated and with which tools.
3. **Labelling of synthetic content** when the film is shared beyond the private sphere.

## Can I give a film as a gift without the other person knowing?

The story can be a surprise. The use of the image cannot. The people who appear must consent. In practice it works like this: the person knows a film exists and authorises the use of their image, but does not know the script until the premiere. Another option is to give the experience as a gift and create the film together.

## What to ask any service that uses your face with AI

Whether it is us or anyone else, these questions should have a clear answer before you send a photo:

- What exactly will my photos be used for?
- Are they used to train models?
- Where are they stored, and for how long?
- Who can see the result, and where will it be published?
- How do I ask for them to be deleted?

If a service cannot answer these questions, be wary.
