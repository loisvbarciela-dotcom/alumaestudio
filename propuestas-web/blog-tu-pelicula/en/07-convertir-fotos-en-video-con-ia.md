# Turning photos into video with AI: free apps or a directed film

Not long ago, turning a photo into a video was a job for visual-effects studios. Today there are apps that, in a few seconds, make a photograph smile, turn its head or walk through a landscape. It is natural to wonder whether that is enough for a gift, a wedding or an anniversary.

This guide explains what options there are for **turning photos into video with AI**, what you can expect from each, what to check before uploading your photos and when it makes sense to commission a directed film.

## Three ways of turning photos into video

### 1. Animating a photo
The simplest tools add movement to a still image: a blink, a smile, a slight turn of the head, a camera moving closer. The result lasts a few seconds and keeps the original framing.

**What it's for:** an emotional keepsake, a greeting, a social media post.

### 2. Generating a clip from an image
Other tools use the photo as a starting point to create a new clip of a few seconds: the person somewhere else, in different clothes, doing something else. This is where the first difficulties appear: the likeness doesn't always hold, and each clip is independent of the last.

**What it's for:** experimenting, creating short content, testing ideas.

### 3. Creating a film from photos
A film is something else: several scenes, characters who must stay the same from start to finish, a story with a beginning and an end, music, rhythm and editing. An app doesn't do that automatically: it takes a script, direction and many shot-by-shot decisions. It is what we do in [Your film](/en/your-film).

## How to get the most out of an app

If you are going to try one yourselves, these tips help:

- **Use a sharp, front-facing, well-lit photo.** The AI invents what it can't see; the more information it has, the better.
- **Ask for simple movements.** Simple gestures work better than complex actions.
- **Generate several versions** and keep the best. The first is rarely the good one.
- **Look after the music and sound**, which are usually what most separates a home video from a moving one.
- **Don't chain too many clips together:** without editing, the accumulation is tiring.

## Before you upload your photos: privacy

A face is personal data. Before uploading photos to any app, check:

- **What its terms say** about the use of the images you upload.
- **Where they are stored** and for how long.
- **Whether you have permission from everyone** who appears in the photo.

If you use someone else's image, ask their permission. We talk about it in [your image and AI](/en/blog/imagen-e-ia-consentimiento-y-derechos).

## What an app doesn't usually solve

- **Character consistency:** the same person being recognisable in every scene.
- **The story:** a sequence of clips is not a narrative.
- **The language of cinema:** considered framing, light, rhythm, transitions.
- **The finish:** music, sound, colour and editing.

It is not a defect of the tools: they are simply built for something else.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you make an AI video from a single photo?
Yes, but the result will be a short clip. For a film with several scenes and two protagonists we work with one photo of each person.

### Which photo gives the best result?
A recent, sharp portrait, with the face well lit and nothing covering the features. More tips in [how to choose the photos](/en/blog/como-elegir-las-fotos-para-vuestra-pelicula).

### Is it legal to turn someone else's photo into a video?
You need their permission. A person's image is protected and, if it is used with AI, transparency matters even more.
