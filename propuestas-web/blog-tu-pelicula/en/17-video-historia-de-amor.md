# How to tell your love story in a video (without falling back on a slideshow)

Every couple has a story. The trouble is that when they try to tell it in a video, it almost always ends up the same way: photos in order, a song and a ‘and then we got married’. Making a good **love story video** is an exercise in scriptwriting, not in collecting.

At ALUMA, we make films. These are the principles we use to tell stories in a few minutes, applied to yours.

## 1. Choose which story to tell

‘Our relationship’ is not a story: it is an archive. A story has a specific moment, a conflict and a change. Some questions to help you find it:

- Was there a moment when everything could have gone another way?
- What had to happen for you to meet?
- What obstacle did you overcome together?
- Is there a phrase, an object or a place that sums it all up?

A good answer to just one of these questions is better material than a hundred photos.

## 2. A simple structure: before, the meeting, after

Even in three minutes, the classic structure works:

1. **Before:** who you were apart. Two lives that have not yet crossed.
2. **The meeting:** the moment they cross. This is the heart of the video.
3. **After:** what you are building. It ends by looking forward, not back.

## 3. Show, don't explain

Cinema tells its story through images. Instead of a caption that says ‘we love travelling’, a suitcase, a platform, a map. Instead of ‘she is always late’, a clock moving on. Visual metaphors let a personal story move people who have never heard it.

## 4. Choose a tone and hold to it

Tender, epic, funny, mysterious? Mixing tones in three minutes confuses the viewer. Decide on one, and let the music, the pace and the images respect it.

## 5. Music is not background

Music sets the rhythm of the edit. Choose a piece whose structure follows the story: let the moment of the meeting coincide with a change in the music, and let the ending arrive with the end of the song.

## 6. Fewer minutes, more emotion

Three or four minutes are enough to tell a complete story without the room reaching for their phones. If you have material to spare, cut it: what remains will be better.

## 7. Sometimes the best way to tell it is to invent it

This is the idea we work with in [Your film](/en/your-film): instead of going over what happened, we tell your story as an allegory. Two people who meet in Ancient Rome, in a twenties ballroom or between planets. It is you, recognisable by your faces, but in a story made for the cinema.

Why does it work? Because the audience recognises the emotional truth (how you look at each other, what binds you) in a setting they have never seen. It is what we do in our short films: small, true stories in extraordinary worlds. You can watch them in [our work](/en/work).

## If you are making it yourselves: a checklist

- One central idea, in a single sentence.
- A structure of before, the meeting, after.
- Three or four minutes at most.
- A single, well-chosen song.
- Minimal on-screen text.
- A review by someone who does not know the story: if they understand it and are moved, it works.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a ‘love story’ video?
A video that tells a couple's love story, usually to be shown at the wedding or shared with family.

### How long should it be?
Between three and four minutes is ideal for showing at an event.

### Can we include real details in an invented film?
Yes. Places, phrases or objects from your story can be woven into the script as quiet nods.
