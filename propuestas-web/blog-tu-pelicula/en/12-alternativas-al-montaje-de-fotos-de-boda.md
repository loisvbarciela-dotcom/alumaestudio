# Beyond the photo montage: alternatives to the classic wedding slideshow

The same photos, the same music, the same edit. The **wedding photo slideshow** is almost a tradition: each person's childhood, adolescence, the first trip together and a song that swells at the end. It moves the family, and that is precisely why it is repeated at nearly every wedding.

There is nothing wrong with doing it. But if you want something different, there are alternatives. And if you do go ahead, there are ways of making it much better.

## Why all slideshows look alike

It isn't that all stories are the same: it is that they are told the same way. A chronological run of photos, with automatic transitions and a song, always produces the same effect. Three reasons:

- **There is no story:** stages are run through, but nothing specific is told.
- **It goes on too long:** with a hundred photos, the room switches off halfway.
- **The photos weren't meant for a big screen:** many are small, blurry or badly framed.

## If you make a slideshow, make it a good one

1. **Three or four minutes at most.** Between thirty and fifty photos, not two hundred.
2. **One idea, not a chronology.** For example: “all the times we nearly never met”.
3. **Brief on-screen text.** A fact or a phrase per section helps the story along.
4. **Music with structure.** Let the most emotional moment coincide with the end of the song, not the middle.
5. **Minimum quality.** Out with pixelated photos and screenshots.

## Alternatives to the photo slideshow

### The interview video
The couple, separately, answer the same questions. It is edited by alternating the answers. Fun and sincere.

### The video from the people who know you
Family and friends each tell an anecdote. It works if limited to five or six people and each speaks for less than thirty seconds.

### The letter read aloud
One of the couple writes a letter to the other, and it is screened with images in the background or read live. Intimate and very powerful.

### Your story told as a fairy tale
Illustrations or simple animation to tell how you met. Lovely and different, though it takes time.

### A film starring the bride and groom
Instead of going over the past, imagine another possible story: the couple in Ancient Rome, in the twenties or in outer space, with their own faces, in a film of three or four minutes with the script, direction and editing of cinema. It starts from two photographs, one of each person, and a conversation about what connects you. It is what we do in [Your film](/en/your-film).

## Photo slideshow vs. personalised film

| | Photo slideshow | Personalised film |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Dozens or hundreds of photos | Two photos and a conversation |
| What it tells | What has already happened | A new story starring the two of you |
| Language | Slideshow | Cinema: scenes, framing, editing |
| Surprise for guests | Low: they have seen it before | High: they haven't seen it at any other wedding |
| Length | Variable | 3–4 minutes |

## What if we don't want to give up the usual photos?

You don't have to choose. Some couples screen a short slideshow for the family during the canapés and keep the film for the reception meal. What matters is that each piece has its own moment and that they aren't chained together. Ideas on timing and technique in [what video to show at a wedding](/en/blog/que-video-proyectar-en-una-boda).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photos should a wedding slideshow have?
Between thirty and fifty for about three or four minutes. More photos doesn't mean more emotion.

### What song should I put on the photo slideshow?
One that means something to you and whose structure follows the story. Check that the volume and the quality of the file are good.

### Does the personalised film use our childhood photos?
No. It starts from one current photo of each person to create the protagonists. If you want to include a nod to your childhood, it can be woven into the script.
