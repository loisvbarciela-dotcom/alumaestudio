# Wedding video or personalised film: the differences and how to combine them

When a couple looks for a **unique wedding video**, they tend to find two worlds that share a name and little else: the traditional wedding video, which records the day, and the personalised film, which is created beforehand and premieres during the wedding. They do not compete. They do different things and, combined, they tell the whole story.

## The wedding video: a memory of what happened

A good wedding videographer records the day with a filmmaker's eye: the preparations, the ceremony, the family's emotions, the party. The result is usually a short highlights film and, sometimes, a longer version.

**What it is for:** remembering how the day was, with real people, real voices and moments that will not come again.

**When it is watched:** weeks or months after the wedding.

**What it needs:** the crew to be present throughout the day.

## The personalised film: a story that premieres

A personalised film is a three- or four-minute piece in which the couple star in a work of fiction: a meeting in Ancient Rome, a dance in the twenties, a journey between planets. It is created before the wedding from two photographs, one of each person, with a script agreed with the couple and the AI directed shot by shot.

**What it is for:** surprising your guests with something they have never seen, and telling your story in a way that cannot be filmed.

**When it is watched:** during the wedding, as a premiere, normally at the reception.

**What it needs:** two photos, a conversation and production time before the date.

## The differences, in a table

| | Wedding video | Personalised film |
|---|---|---|
| What it tells | What happened on the wedding day | An imagined story with you as the protagonists |
| When it is made | On the day itself | Before the wedding |
| When it is watched | After the wedding | During the wedding (premiere) |
| Starting material | Real footage shot on the day | Two photos and a conversation |
| Usual length | Variable | 3–4 minutes |
| Who sees it first | The couple | All the guests at once |
| What it brings | Memory | Surprise and storytelling |

## How to combine them

- **The film opens, the video closes.** The film premieres at the reception; the videographer films the guests' reaction, which ends up as part of the wedding video. It is one of the most moving scenes you can capture.
- **Coordinating your suppliers.** If you work with a wedding planner, they will fit the screening into the running order of the day. If not, speak to the venue and the DJ. Technical ideas in [which video to screen at a wedding](/en/blog/que-video-proyectar-en-una-boda).
- **Visual coherence.** You can ask for the film and the video to share references: a song, a colour palette, a title.

## And the photo slideshow?

It is the third classic piece, and many couples replace it with the film. If you want both, put them at different moments. More ideas in [alternatives to the wedding photo slideshow](/en/blog/alternativas-al-montaje-de-fotos-de-boda).

## If you are a wedding professional

For videographers and wedding planners, the personalised film is not competition: it is a complement you can offer to couples. We talk about it in [wedding planners and personalised experiences](/en/blog/wedding-planners-experiencias-personalizadas).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the film replace the wedding video?
No. The video records the real day; the film is a work of fiction that premieres during the wedding. They complement each other.

### Do we need to film anything for the film?
No. We start from one photograph of each person.

### Can the videographer film the premiere?
Yes, and we recommend it: the reaction of the room is one of the best moments of the day.
