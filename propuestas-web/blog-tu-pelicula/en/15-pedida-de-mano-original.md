# Original proposal ideas: how to pop the question with a film

A proposal lasts a few seconds and is remembered for a lifetime. That is why it is so hard to decide how to do it. The ideas that circulate on social media — the ring in the glass, the banner in the sky, the flash mob — have lost their surprise through repetition. An **original proposal** doesn't have to be spectacular: it has to be yours.

Here are ideas for every style, advice almost nobody gives and a way of asking that nobody has seen: at the end of a film starring you both.

## First of all: three tips that matter more than the idea

1. **Think about who the other person is.** Do they like surprises in public, or would they hate it? A proposal that is perfect for an extrovert can be an ordeal for someone shy.
2. **Choose a place with meaning**, not just a pretty one: where you met, where the first date was, somewhere that unites you.
3. **Have a plan for afterwards.** A reserved dinner, the family waiting or, simply, a bottle of something good.

## Original proposal ideas

### Going back to where it began
Recreate the first date: same place, same time, same dish. The question arrives where it all began.

### The treasure hunt
Clues that run through important places in your story and end with the question. It takes preparation, but you will enjoy it for hours.

### Proposing in nature
A viewpoint, a lighthouse, a beach at sunset. In Galicia, a walk along the cliffs or a route through the Ribeira Sacra has everything you need.

### The letter or the book
A small book with your story whose last page is the question.

### The film with a surprise ending
The most cinematic idea: a film of three or four minutes in which the two of you are the protagonists, living your love in another era or another world (Ancient Rome, the twenties, outer space, a lighthouse at the end of the world). The story moves on, the room falls silent and, in the last scene, the question appears.

## How a proposal film works

From two photographs, one of each person, and a conversation with the person who is going to propose, we write a script that leads up to the question. Then we direct the artificial intelligence shot by shot and edit the film. The premiere can be at home, at a dinner with friends or in a room hired for the occasion.

### Surprise and consent
We must be honest here. A personalised film uses the image of both people, and **we always work with the explicit consent of both**. That doesn't prevent the surprise: the other person can know that a film about you exists — a gift, a couple's project — and authorise the use of their image without knowing the script. What they don't know is how it ends.

If you would rather they knew nothing, there is another option: propose in your own way and then create **the engagement film**, to premiere in front of the family or to use as an announcement of the wedding. Ideas in [save the date videos](/en/blog/save-the-date-en-video).

## Universes that work well for a proposal

- **The twenties:** a railway station, a last train, a question before departure.
- **The lighthouse:** two people searching for each other in the storm.
- **Outer space:** a crossing between worlds that ends in a single question.
- **Your universe:** the place where you met turned into a film set.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best date to propose?
The one that makes sense for you: an anniversary, a trip, an ordinary day chosen on purpose. Christmas and Valentine's Day are popular, but also predictable.

### How do I keep the surprise if their consent is needed?
Their consent is for appearing in a film; the script and the ending can stay secret.
